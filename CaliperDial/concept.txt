2. The Sunbeam / Crystal Deflector (Laser Routing)Temples love mirrors. But instead of the standard 45-degree mirror puzzle everyone has played, the mirrors here behave according to wave and projection mechanics.The Core Action: A shaft of sunlight cuts into the ceiling. You place and orient rotating obsidian mirrors, prisms, and shadow-casting rods to hit crystal sensors that open stone doors.The Identity Trick: A sensor sits behind an obstruction that blocks direct light.Negative angles ($\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta, \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$): Aiming the beam down into an underground trench reaches the exact same horizontal distance along the floor as aiming it up toward the ceiling, but flips the vertical receiver from positive to negative.Double angles: To trigger a high-frequency crystal that only vibrates to a double-angle sweep, the player combines two single-angle crystal lenses. The game visually merges two light beams into one concentrated beam that sweeps across the target at double speed.Why a 10-Year-Old Plays: Light puzzles provide instant, vivid visual feedback. Dark rooms light up, glowing runes ignite along the walls, and lasers shatter brittle stone barriers.
